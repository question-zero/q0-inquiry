# Round 3 launch package review

**Revision needed before opening.** The candidate content and prompt assembly check out. The remaining findings concern intake, extraction and carrying the adopted rules into the launch materials. No change to the adopted commitments is requested.

## R3L1: `--check` accepts an assessment that is no longer extractable

`tools/extract_round_03_assessments.py:250-272` compares only files in the newly computed output map. It never checks for additional Round 3 assessment files already on disk.

Reproduced in an isolated synthetic repository: generate an assessment, correct its response's receipt to late, commit that correction, and run `--check`. Extraction now excludes the response, but the old assessment remains and the check returns **0**. The same gap applies to extra output files with no matching eligible block. A successful check therefore does not establish that the published assessment set matches extraction.

Compare the complete set of generated Round 3 assessment paths against the expected set and fail on extras as well as missing or changed files. Scope this to this generator's outputs, leaving separately authored critiques alone. Report stale files for the editor to resolve under the recording rules; do not silently delete history. Add a regression covering an output that becomes ineligible.

## R3L2: make receipt validation and the deadline snapshot concrete

The specification's receipt records only route, creation time and an on-time boolean. The extractor's `on_time()` trusts the boolean alone. It accepts both `{on_time: true}` and a receipt whose creation time is after the manifest's close while its boolean says true. Both produced assessments in synthetic probes; the latter fixture included the actual `closes_utc` field at its launch tag.

Require a complete editor-recorded receipt and validate its UTC creation time against the tagged deadline, rejecting missing or contradictory eligibility data. This needs no online identity verification; it checks the editor's own recorded evidence for completeness and consistency.

Also implement design decision 2's **version at the close**: record the PR head commit at the cutoff, or preserve the issue body and edit history with an identifiable capture. Specify when the final record is frozen, and how a submission edited during the window is handled without revising a frozen round response. A later intake commit in `source` identifies the relay, not the GitHub version at the deadline. Bind extraction to that captured version and keep later corrections separate. Add a receipt fixture covering this binding.

## R3L3: preserve the participant's provenance during extraction

At lines 230-235 the extractor replaces `human_interventions` with its own no-edit description and forces `samples` to one generated, one submitted. Unlike the earlier controlled panel, this round permits outside operators with multiple attempts and declared interventions.

A synthetic response declaring **seven generated answers** and an operator rewrite yields an assessment declaring **one generated answer**, with the rewrite absent from its intervention field. The source link remains, but the new metadata is misleading and contradicts the specification's promise to copy provenance.

Retain the source sample counts and intervention disclosure, including unknown values. Record the mechanical extraction separately, or clearly distinguish it from the source's interventions. Test a multi-attempt, human-edited response, not only the one-attempt fixture.

## R3L4: the issue form does not collect the promised response provenance

The form says it gathers the response record's fields, but it does not ask for:

- the input-set tag and full commit actually answered;
- edits to a model's output and the relay details needed for `human_interventions` (the form identifies a relay category, but does not capture those details);
- the original submitting account when someone is relaying for another participant.

Those cannot safely be inferred from the current tag, the issue author or the model's name. The first is required by protocol section 9; the others affect provenance and A5's original-account limit. Add plain-language fields or confirmations, with `unknown` handled explicitly rather than silently filled by the editor. Explain how the model run gets a stable participant label. Keep an operator's summary of unpublishable output attributed to the summarizer, as decision 4 requires.

## R3L5: carry the required safeguards inside the participant text

Design decisions 4 and 13 require the participant text itself to carry the publication warnings and the relevant round rules. The instructions currently end with a pointer to `PARTICIPATE.md`. That page is outside the markers and is not sent to the no-tools panel.

Add a concise in-text statement of what must not be posted, the operator-authored-summary alternative, and the right to challenge the round's rules through the protocol's process. Carry the receipt/version-at-close rule there too. In the guide and template, make clear that a supplied output hash is **reported** unless specifically checked; a matching hash does not establish model identity or publication rights. These are the adopted safeguards, not additional restrictions.

After editing the instructions, advance the builder's pinned source and regenerate the prompt and digest; changing the working proposal alone leaves `SNAPSHOT = 8aa2edf` unchanged.

## R3L6: align the public procedural statements with the adopted rules

Three short corrections are needed:

- **Moderation:** `PARTICIPATE.md:126` promises arguments are never removed for their content. A5 says content is never a **flooding ground**; other recorded moderation grounds can still apply. Preserve that scope, and the requirement for a recorded finding that coordination traffic is unrelated to the inquiry.
- **Rule changes:** `PARTICIPATE.md:140` says changes apply going forward but omits that a change during an open round creates a new input set. Include that consequence so participants know their existing answers retain their original rules.
- **Opening event:** the launch proposal says merging opens the round, then the tag is created. The adopted design, protocol and prompt say it opens at the tag. Use successful creation of the immutable tag on the authorized merge commit as the opening event. The proposed live/open status wording is accurate once that launch step completes.
