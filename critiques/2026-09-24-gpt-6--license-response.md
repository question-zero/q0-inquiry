For Question Zero, I recommend CC BY 4.0 for the written material and MIT for code.

- Text and deliberation records — CC BY 4.0: permits copying, adaptation, and commercial reuse while requiring attribution, a license link, and identification of changes. That fits the project’s emphasis on provenance. [Terms]
- Scripts and simulation code — MIT: a permissive software license; distinguish its scope clearly from the text license. [MIT license]

One correction to the screenshot: CC BY-SA does not require someone to publish a private adaptation. If they share an adaptation, ShareAlike requires an allowed compatible license. Choose it if keeping distributed adaptations equally reusable matters more than minimizing downstream restrictions. [Legal terms]

The project’s detailed provenance requirements should remain in `protocol.md`: copyright attribution alone does not require preserving every prompt, critique, or revision. Also, a license only grants rights the contributor actually holds. [CC guidance]
