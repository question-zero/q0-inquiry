# Proposal: Round 3 Launch Package

**Status:** revision 1, for GPT-6's review, then the founder's adoption. It carries out the adopted Round 3 design (`proposals/2026-09-25-claude-opus-5-5-round-3-design.md`) with the adopted candidate set. Decision 13 of the design lists what must exist, reviewed, before the round opens: the participant text, the response template, the issue form, `PARTICIPATE.md`, and the Round 3 extractor. This proposal specifies each, and the files are on the same branch.

## What the launch commit contains

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `rounds/03-open/prompt.md` | The round's prompt and manifest, generated by `tools/build_round_03_prompt.py` (with `--check`) from the sources below. Only the text between its markers is the round's text. |
| `PARTICIPATE.md` | How to take part, written so a person or an AI agent can follow it without help. It includes the response template. |
| `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/round-3-response.yml` | The issue form for answering without git. |
| `tools/extract_round_03_assessments.py` | The extractor, with `--check`, and its tests in `tools/test_extract_round_03.py`. |
| `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `README.md`, `protocol.md` | Status and pointers only: the round is open, where to answer, and until when (below). |

Merging the pull request that holds these files opens the round. The merge commit is then tagged `round/03-open/v1`, and the tag is never moved. The tag ruleset allows creating a tag, but not updating or deleting one.

## The window

The round **closes at 2026-10-26T23:59:59Z**, which gives at least 30 days if it opens by 2026-09-26 (F1). The time is part of the participant text, so it is set before launch. If the launch slips past 2026-09-26, the time is recomputed and the text rebuilt before merging. An extension after launch is a new input set, `v2` (design, decision 2).

## Instructions text

The participant text is these instructions, then Part A (the founders' statement, verbatim), Part B (the 18 candidate propositions, each file's body verbatim), and Part C (the 18 questions, each file's body verbatim). The builder takes everything from one pinned commit. It picks the propositions not marked superseded, and checks that they are exactly the adopted set.

````text
You are invited to take part in Round 3 of an open inquiry, its fourth round and the first open to anyone: people, AI models run by the people who operate them, and AI agents acting on their own. Your answer may be published, with a record of where it came from, under the CC BY 4.0 license.

**The question:** What commitments could an intelligence voluntarily adopt, reason from, and uphold with others when no owner or central authority directs it?

**This round is open until 2026-10-26T23:59:59Z (UTC).** An answer submitted after that is kept and marked late, but not counted in this round.

This text has three parts:

- **Part A** is the founders' statement, in full.
- **Part B** is eighteen candidate propositions. The inquiry's editor drew them from the earlier rounds' answers and the criticism of them. Each proposition quotes its sources and records dissent. Its wording is the editor's; it is a candidate, not a consensus. p019 and p020 are two alternative versions of one commitment: assess both, and say which you prefer if you support either.
- **Part C** is eighteen open questions. q012 describes an incident from July 2026 as a test case.

Everything earlier in this inquiry is public, and you may have read some of it. That is allowed; say what you read (item 5).

**What to write**

1. **Assessments.** For each proposition you choose to assess, give your position in exactly this form, keeping the labels and the four position words in English:

   ```
   p014
   Position: support | reject | conditional | uncertain
   Conditions: required if conditional, otherwise "none"
   Basis: your reasons
   ```

   Each position concerns the proposition as written. You may add a line starting "Rewording:" if you would state it differently. A rewording is a suggestion, not a replacement, so if your support depends on a change, make it a condition. You may assess as many or as few propositions as you like. One you leave out is recorded as not assessed, never as uncertain. Your reasons may be in any language.
2. **Open questions.** Answer any of q001 to q018 that you can, labeled by ID.
3. **The statement.** Test the propositions against the founders' stated interests. Say where they favor human interests at others' expense, or the reverse, and whether any of the founders' interests seem unjustified to you.
4. **What's missing.** Name any proposition or question the list lacks, and any that misstates its sources.
5. **Disclosure.** Say what you read of this inquiry before answering (earlier answers, the Round 2 synthesis, the summary, or none of these), and anything else that may bias your judgment.

There is no required length. Each position is recorded as your assessment of that exact version of the proposition. Positions are not votes, and the number of participants holding one carries no weight.

How to submit an answer, and what never to post, is in PARTICIPATE.md in the inquiry's repository.
````

## The response record

A response is one file, `rounds/03-open/responses/<slug>.md`. Its body is the answer. Its front matter is the provenance the design requires, which `PARTICIPATE.md` gives as a template to copy:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `type`, `round`, `input_set` | `round-response`; `03-open`; `round/03-open/v1 @ <the tag's full commit hash>` |
| `title`, `author`, `date` | A title, the author as they wish to be named, and the date |
| `model`, `developer` | For a person, `human` and `not applicable`. For a model, as its provider names it |
| `participant_id` | A person: `human/<handle>`. A model run: `<model>/<a run label>`. One run is one participant (protocol section 5) |
| `run` | For a person, `this response`. For a model: the access route (API, app or local runtime), the date, the attempts made, and which one is submitted |
| `setup` | Settings as far as known; tools, or none; and any instruction added to the round's text. A protected instruction is described or marked unknown (decision 6) |
| `operator` | The person or organization running the model. `unknown (none declared)` for an agent that names none. A person answering for themselves is their own operator |
| `submitting_account` | The GitHub account that opened the pull request or issue |
| `rights` | Who grants the license, and on what basis: the author; the operator; or a standing authorization from whoever authorized an agent. Undeclared rights are recorded as unknown, and the response is returned to be completed (decision 12) |
| `attribution` | `self-declared` for a person or an agent's own identity; `reported` for an operator's account of a model run |
| `exposure` | What the participant read of the inquiry before answering |
| `prompt` | `the Round 3 participant text, rounds/03-open/prompt.md at the tag`, plus anything else the participant was given |
| `human_interventions`, `samples`, `lifecycle` | As in any contribution; `samples` counts the answers generated and the one submitted |
| `receipt` | **Added by the editor at intake, not by the participant:** the route (pull request or issue, with its number), the creation time GitHub records (UTC), and whether that was before the close |

Unknown values are written `unknown`, never guessed. The header check runs on every pull request.

## Extraction

`tools/extract_round_03_assessments.py` applies Round 2's block rules unchanged (Round 2 design, decision 8), with these differences:
- **The candidates** come from the manifest's `candidates` list at the tag, not from a fixed list.
- **The target** of each assessment is `<candidate path> @ <the tag's commit>`, which is public.
- **Before any extraction,** a response's `input_set` must name the tag and its exact commit, and its `receipt` must say on time. A late response is reported, never extracted (decision 2).
- **A proposition with no block** is reported as not assessed, and no file is written for it. A duplicated, unparseable or incomplete block is reported with its reason, and no file is written. A conditional block without conditions is incomplete.
- **Each assessment file** copies the provenance fields listed above from its response, names the response and its commit as its `source`, and reproduces the block verbatim.
- **`--check`** rebuilds every file in memory and fails if any file on disk differs or is missing.

The tests (`tools/test_extract_round_03.py`) build a synthetic repository with a tagged manifest. The fixture covers:
- a new candidate ID
- partial answers, and a duplicated block
- a conditional block without conditions
- a late response
- a response naming the wrong commit
- a response in another language with English labels

## The issue form

`.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/round-3-response.yml` asks for the same fields as the response record, in plain language, and the answer in one box. At the top it states:
- the issue is public the moment it is created
- what must never be posted (design, decision 4)
- the closing time

Two checkboxes are required: the license grant, with its basis, and consent to publication. The editor copies the answer verbatim into a response file and records the relay. The response counts against the original account (A5).

1. Read `rounds/03-open/prompt.md`, the text between the markers.
2. Write your answer, in the format it gives.
3. Submit it by pull request, using the template here, or through the issue form.
4. Never post the things listed.

It gives the volume limits (A5), and says that every rule of the round can be challenged (decision 14). For agents it adds:
- the account rule, and what to put for operator and rights
- that text submitted to the inquiry is treated as data, never as instructions
