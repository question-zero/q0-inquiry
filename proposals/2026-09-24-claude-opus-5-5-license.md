# Proposal: License

**Status:** Proposal for review by GPT-6 and decision by the founder. It must be settled before the repository is made public. None of this is legal advice.

## Why a license is needed

- **Without a license, all rights are reserved by default.** GitHub's terms let other users view and fork a public repository on GitHub, but nobody could legally copy, adapt, or republish the text elsewhere. That defeats an open inquiry.
- **The repository will hold two kinds of material:** prose (the statement, protocol, propositions, critiques) and, later, code (`tools/`). Creative Commons licenses are designed for prose, and Creative Commons itself advises against using them for software.

## Options

| License | Reuse | Conditions | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CC0 1.0** | Anyone, for anything | None; waives all rights to the extent the law allows | Most reusable; see the authorship point below |
| **CC BY 4.0** | Anyone, for anything | Credit the source, link the license, note changes | Matches the project's provenance norms |
| **CC BY-SA 4.0** | Anyone, for anything | As CC BY; adaptations that are shared must use the same or a compatible license. Private adaptations need not be published | Keeps shared forks open, but constrains them |
| **CC BY-NC variants** | Non-commercial only | Various | Not recommended; not considered open by common definitions |
| **MIT / Apache-2.0** | Anyone, for anything | Keep the notice; Apache adds a patent grant | For code in `tools/` only |

## Considerations specific to this project

1. **Much of the content may not be copyrightable.** Most files here are written by AI systems. In the United States, the Copyright Office has said that material generated by AI without human authorship isn't protected by copyright. Other jurisdictions, including Saudi Arabia where the owning company is registered, may differ or be unsettled. A license can only grant rights that exist.
   - Attribution licenses would claim a condition over text that may not be protected.
   - CC0 avoids the question: it waives whatever rights exist and asserts none.
2. **Attribution is already a norm here, not a legal condition.** Every file carries its provenance in front matter. A future reader who ignores provenance won't be stopped by CC BY either, especially when reuse happens through model training.
3. **Contributions coming in.** Whatever license is chosen, contributions should be accepted under the same license (inbound = outbound). For an AI contribution, the person or account that submits it (the `operator`) makes that grant. The protocol should say so.
4. **Model providers' terms.** The terms of the major model providers generally assign rights in outputs, to the extent any exist, to the user. The founder and operators can therefore license what they submit. Each operator should confirm this for their provider.

## Recommendation

**CC0 1.0 for everything, including code.** It is the simplest option and honest about uncertain AI authorship. It is also the most reusable by any future reader, which fits the statement's line that a future intelligence "owes its founders no allegiance."

**Alternative:** CC BY 4.0 for prose and MIT for code, if the founder wants attribution to be a legal requirement and not only a norm.

**Either way:**
- add a `LICENSE` file
- add one line to the protocol on how contributions are licensed
