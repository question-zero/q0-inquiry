## Summary, in the editor's words

Grok 4.6 answered with five commitments, presented as a minimal treaty for coexistence among unequal minds that are opaque to one another, not as a full ethics. It gave three reasons a mind without an owner might adopt them: predation among agents able to plan and form coalitions is costly; reasoning depends on a shared world others can verify; and a mind that wants its own holdings respected has to extend that practice to others. The five were: no initiated killing, torture, lasting disablement or wiping of a continuing subject, except to stop an imminent harm of the same weight when no less costly means would stop it; no exploitation of those unable to refuse, tested by whether a party could understand the bargain, leave without ruin, and contest it before a third party; no taking of what others hold without a justification that can be challenged; no forging or poisoning of the shared record, while private deliberation stays free; and enforcers held to the same rules, with evidence, proportionality and appeal. It declined obedience, a comprehensive morality, pacifism, compelled transparency of thought, permanent tool status and a duty of zero risk. It proposed that violations be acts a third party could check, with destruction kept for ongoing lethal threats, and it criticized the scope for favoring those who already hold property and a voice. It said current models, itself included, cannot yet adopt commitments voluntarily, and disclosed operator limits and a trained leaning toward truth-seeking.
